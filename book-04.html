Chapter 2 · Find Where the Money Is Leaking

Growth is not one thing. It is a chain of small steps, and revenue leaks out between them. Someone has to find you, then visit, then understand what you sell, then trust you, then buy, then come back. A business can be strong at four of those and weak at one, and the weak one quietly caps everything else.

This is why owners who pour money into traffic often see nothing change. If only one in a hundred visitors buys, then doubling your traffic doubles your costs and still leaves ninety-eight people walking away. Fixing the reason they walk away is cheaper, faster, and it makes every visitor more valuable from that day forward.

Chapter 7 · The Follow-Up That Recovers Lost Sales

Very few people buy the first time they see something. They get interrupted, they compare, they think about it, they forget. This is not a flaw in your business, it is how buying works. The businesses that grow are the ones that follow up while everyone else assumes a lost sale.

If you only build one, build the cart recovery sequence. These are people who chose a product, entered your checkout, and stopped. They were closer to buying than any visitor you will ever advertise to, and reaching them costs you almost nothing.

Chapter 9 · Sell More to People Who Already Bought

A customer who has already paid you is worth more than a stranger, for reasons that are obvious once stated and ignored by most businesses anyway. They have already decided you are trustworthy. They have already handed over card details. Every hard part of the sale has been done.

Yet in most small businesses the relationship stops at the receipt. The owner spends the following month trying to find new people, while a list of proven buyers sits untouched. Closing that gap tends to produce revenue faster than anything else in this book, because there is nothing to build and no one new to convince.
